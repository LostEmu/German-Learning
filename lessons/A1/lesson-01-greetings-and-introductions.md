## 1. Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

1. **Greet** people and **say goodbye** appropriately for the time of day and the situation.
2. Understand the **du/Sie distinction** (informal vs. formal "you"): what it means socially, when to use which, and what it changes grammatically.
3. **Introduce yourself**: your name, where you are from, where you live, what you study.
4. **Ask others** for the same information, both formally and informally.
5. Ask **how someone is** and answer.
6. Use **survival expressions**: please, thank you, sorry, pardon, "I don't understand".
7. Recognise the **verb-second rule** in statements and W-questions.

**Why this matters:** these are the first sentences you will say in any German-speaking environment. More importantly, choosing between *du* and *Sie* is a decision you'll make in **every** conversation in German for the rest of your life. Getting it wrong is not a small grammar slip: it sends a social signal (over-familiar or cold).

## 2. Concept 1: Greetings depend on time and register

| German | English | Register | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hallo!** | Hello / Hi | Neutral–informal | Any time; fine with most people, though a little casual for very formal settings |
| **Guten Morgen!** | Good morning | Neutral–formal | Until about 10–11 am |
| **Guten Tag!** | Good day / Hello | Neutral–formal | Daytime. The safe formal greeting (shops, offices) |
| **Guten Abend!** | Good evening | Neutral–formal | From about 6 pm |
| **Gute Nacht!** | Good night | Any | **Only** when someone goes to bed or you part late at night. **Never** as a greeting on arrival |

| German | English | Register |
|---|---|---|
| **Tschüss!** (also spelled *Tschüs*) | Bye! | Informal |
| **Auf Wiedersehen!** | Goodbye (lit. "until seeing again") | Formal / neutral |
| **Bis bald!** | See you soon | Neutral–informal |
| **Bis morgen!** | See you tomorrow | Neutral |

### Why *Guten* Morgen and not *Gut* Morgen?
These greetings are shortened from a full sentence: *(Ich wünsche Ihnen einen) guten Morgen*, "(I wish you a) good morning". The greeting is grammatically the **object** of an unspoken verb "wish". It is therefore in the **accusative case**, and the adjective *gut* takes an ending: **guten** (masculine, because *der Morgen* is masculine). *Gute Nacht* has **-e** because *die Nacht* is feminine. You do not need to analyse this yet. Cases come in Lessons 7–8, adjective endings at A2. Learn these as fixed expressions for now.

> 🇨🇭 **Zurich preview (recognition only):** in Zurich you will constantly hear **Grüezi** (formal "hello", Swiss German) and **Hoi** or **Sali** (informal "hi"). These are dialect, not Standard German. We will study Swiss German from B1/B2. For now, just recognise them.

## 3. Concept 2: *du* vs. *Sie*: two words for "you"

### 3.1 What it represents
English once had this distinction too: *thou* (informal, singular) vs. *you* (formal). English lost it; German kept it. Every time you address someone in German, you must choose:

| | **du** | **Sie** |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | informal "you" (one person) | formal "you" (one **or** more people) |
| Signals | closeness, equality, familiarity | respect, professional distance |
| Use with | family, friends, children, animals; **fellow students** and young people of similar age; most online communities | adults you don't know; shop/café staff; officials; **professors and lecturers**; landlords; anyone you address as *Herr/Frau + surname* |
| Name used | first name | *Herr* / *Frau* + surname |
| Capitalisation | lowercase *du* | **always capital *Sie***, even mid-sentence |

### 3.2 Rules of thumb
1. **When in doubt with an adult, use *Sie*.** Being too formal is a minor awkwardness; being too familiar can come across as rude.
2. **Switching to *du* is offered**, usually by the older or more senior person (*Wollen wir uns duzen?*, "Shall we say du to each other?"). Don't switch on your own with a superior.
3. **Among students, *du* is the norm.** At ETH, students say *du* to each other from the first moment. Professors are addressed with *Sie*. In many research groups colleagues use *du*, but wait for it to be offered.
4. Verbs: **duzen** = to address someone with *du*; **siezen** = to address someone with *Sie*.

### 3.3 What it changes grammatically
The verb ending changes to match the pronoun:

| | heißen (to be called) | kommen (to come) | wohnen (to live) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **du** | du heiß**t** | du komm**st** | du wohn**st** |
| **Sie** | Sie heiß**en** | Sie komm**en** | Sie wohn**en** |

**Why does *Sie* take *-en*?** Historically, formal *Sie* is the third-person plural pronoun *sie* ("they"), used as a polite, indirect way of addressing someone. So it takes the "they" verb form, which for regular verbs is identical to the infinitive. That is also why it is capitalised: to distinguish it from *sie* = they.

**Why is it *du heißt* and not *du heißst*?** The *du* ending is *-st*, but when the verb stem already ends in an s-sound (s, ß, z, x), the *s* of the ending merges into it: heiß- + -st → heiß**t**.

## 4. Concept 3: First verb forms: *ich*, *du*, *Sie*

Each German verb has an **infinitive** (the dictionary form, almost always ending in **-en**) and **conjugated forms** that change with the subject. Only the forms needed for introductions are introduced here; the full system is Lesson 2.

| Infinitive | Meaning | ich (I) | du (you, inf.) | Sie (you, formal) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **heißen** | to be called | ich heiß**e** | du heiß**t** | Sie heiß**en** |
| **kommen** | to come | ich komm**e** | du komm**st** | Sie komm**en** |
| **wohnen** | to live (reside) | ich wohn**e** | du wohn**st** | Sie wohn**en** |
| **studieren** | to study (at university) | ich studier**e** | du studier**st** | Sie studier**en** |
| **verstehen** | to understand | ich versteh**e** | du versteh**st** | Sie versteh**en** |
| **sein** | to be | ich **bin** | du **bist** | Sie **sind** |

**Notice the pattern:** remove *-en* from the infinitive to get the **stem** (komm-), then add **-e** (ich), **-st** (du), **-en** (Sie). This is the regular present tense. *sein* is irregular (as "to be" is in English and French), so its forms must be memorised.

**Pronunciation notes:** *studieren* [ʃtuˈdiːʁən]: st- = "sht", ie = long "ee", stress on *-die-*. *verstehen* [fɛɐ̯ˈʃteːən]: ver- unstressed, st = "sht", silent h.

## 5. Concept 4: Word order: the verb is in position 2

**The rule:** in a German statement and in a W-question, the **conjugated verb is the second element** of the sentence.

An "element" (a *position*) is a unit of meaning, which can be **more than one word**:

| Position 1 | Position 2 (verb) | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| Ich | **heiße** | Alex. |
| **Mein Name** | **ist** | Alex. |
| Ich | **komme** | aus Australien. |

*Mein Name* is two words but one element, so the verb is still in position 2.

### W-questions (question words: *wie, wo, woher* …)
The question word takes position 1, the verb stays in position 2, and so the **subject moves after the verb**:

| Position 1 | Position 2 (verb) | Subject | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wie** | **heißt** | du? | |
| **Woher** | **kommen** | Sie? | |
| **Wo** | **wohnst** | du? | |
| **Wie** | **geht** | es | Ihnen? |

**Compare English:** "Where **do** you come from?" German never uses a helper like *do*: **Woher kommst du?** = literally "Where-from come you?" Also, English splits *where … from*; German fuses them into one word: **woher**.

> This verb-second rule (V2) is **the** central rule of German word order. Lesson 6 develops it fully.

### Question words introduced

| Word | Meaning | Asks about |
|---|---|---|
| **wie** | how | manner; also names: *Wie heißt du?* (lit. "How are you called?") |
| **wo** | where | location: *Wo wohnst du?* |
| **woher** | where … from | origin: *Woher kommst du?* |

⚠️ *Wie heißt du?*, **not** ~~*Was heißt du?*~~. *heißen* means "to be called", so German asks *how* you are called, not *what*.
⚠️ *Woher kommst du?*, **not** ~~*Wo kommst du?*~~. *wo* = location only.

## 6. Concept 5: Fixed chunks you will understand later

Some essential phrases contain grammar you haven't learned. Learn them as **chunks** now; their structure will be explained later.

| Chunk | Literal structure | Explained in |
|---|---|---|
| **Wie geht es dir?** (informal) / **Wie geht es Ihnen?** (formal): "How are you?" | "How goes it to-you?" *es* = dummy subject "it"; *geht* from *gehen* (to go); *dir/Ihnen* = "to you" in the **dative case** | Lesson 22 (dative pronouns) |
| **Wie geht's?** | Contraction of *Wie geht es?*. Informal, very common | (now) |
| **Ich komme aus der Schweiz.**: "I'm from Switzerland." | Most countries have no article (*aus Australien, aus Deutschland*), but *die Schweiz* always does, and after *aus* it becomes *der* (dative) | Lesson 24 (dative prepositions) |
| **Freut mich!**: "Pleased to meet you!" | "(It) pleases me!" | (now) |

## 8. Example dialogues

### Dialogue A: informal (two students at a welcome event)

| German | English |
|---|---|
| **Lena:** Hallo! Ich bin Lena. Wie heißt du? | Hi! I'm Lena. What's your name? |
| **Alex:** Hallo, Lena! Ich heiße Alex. | Hi, Lena! My name's Alex. |
| **Lena:** Freut mich, Alex! Woher kommst du? | Nice to meet you, Alex! Where are you from? |
| **Alex:** Ich komme aus Australien. Und du? | I'm from Australia. And you? |
| **Lena:** Ich komme aus Österreich. Wo wohnst du? | I'm from Austria. Where do you live? |
| **Alex:** Ich wohne in Zürich. Ich studiere Physik. | I live in Zurich. I study physics. |
| **Lena:** Ich studiere auch Physik! | I study physics too! |
| **Alex:** Super! … Tschüss, Lena! Bis morgen! | Great! … Bye, Lena! See you tomorrow! |
| **Lena:** Tschüss! | Bye! |

### Dialogue B: formal (at a university office)

| German | English |
|---|---|
| **Frau Keller:** Guten Tag! | Good afternoon! |
| **Alex:** Guten Tag! Mein Name ist Alex Taylor. | Good afternoon! My name is Alex Taylor. |
| **Frau Keller:** Wie bitte? Wie heißen Sie? | Pardon? What's your name? |
| **Alex:** Taylor. Te – a – ypsilon – el – o – er. | Taylor. T – A – Y – L – O – R. |
| **Frau Keller:** Danke. Woher kommen Sie? | Thank you. Where are you from? |
| **Alex:** Ich komme aus Australien. | I'm from Australia. |
| **Frau Keller:** Und wo wohnen Sie? | And where do you live? |
| **Alex:** Ich wohne in Zürich. | I live in Zurich. |
| **Frau Keller:** Gut, danke schön. Auf Wiedersehen! | Good, thank you very much. Goodbye! |
| **Alex:** Bitte schön. Auf Wiedersehen! | You're welcome. Goodbye! |

**Grammar breakdown:**
- *Wie heißen Sie?*: Frau Keller uses **Sie** (stranger, official context), so the verb has **-en**.
- *Woher kommst du?* vs. *Woher kommen Sie?*: same question, different register, different ending.
- *Ich studiere auch Physik.*: verb in position 2; *auch* ("too") comes after the verb, **not** at the end as in English.
- *Te – a – ypsilon – el – o – er*: spelling with German letter names (Lesson 0).

## 10. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why | Code |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Was heißt du?* | *Wie heißt du?* | *heißen* = "to be called": German asks *how* | VOC |
| *Wo kommst du?* | *Woher kommst du?* | origin needs *woher*; *wo* = location | VOC |
| *Woher kommst Sie?* / *Wie heißen du?* | *Woher kommen Sie?* / *Wie heißt du?* | the verb ending must match the pronoun | VERB |
| *Ich bin ein Student.* | *Ich bin Student.* | no article with professions/roles after *sein* | IDIOM |
| *Wie heißen sie?* (to a stranger) | *Wie heißen Sie?* | lowercase *sie* = they/she | SPELL |
| *Gute Nacht!* on arriving at 8 pm | *Guten Abend!* | *Gute Nacht* is only for parting/bedtime | IDIOM |
| *Wie geht es dir, Herr Weber?* | *Wie geht es Ihnen, Herr Weber?* | *Herr/Frau* + surname → *Sie* register | REG |
| *Ich studiere Physik auch.* | *Ich studiere auch Physik.* | *auch* normally comes directly before the element it refers to; at the end of the sentence (English-style) it sounds unnatural | WO |
| *Du kommst woher?* as a neutral question | *Woher kommst du?* | question word first, verb second | WO |

## 11. Summary and revision notes

- **Greetings:** *Guten Morgen / Tag / Abend* (neutral–formal), *Hallo* (informal); *Gute Nacht* only when parting at night.
- **Farewells:** *Auf Wiedersehen* (formal), *Tschüss* (informal), *Bis bald / Bis morgen*.
- **du** = informal, first names, fellow students; **Sie** = formal, *Herr/Frau* + surname, always capitalised. If in doubt with an adult: *Sie*.
- **Verb endings:** stem + **-e** (ich), **-st** (du), **-en** (Sie). *sein*: bin / bist / sind. *heißen*: du heißt (s-sound stems take only -t).
- **Word order:** the conjugated verb is element **2**. In W-questions: question word (1) → verb (2) → subject.
- **wie / wo / woher** = how / where / where from.
- **Learn as chunks:** *Wie geht es dir/Ihnen?*, *aus der Schweiz*, *Guten Tag*.
- **Every noun = article + noun + plural.** Today: der Morgen, der Tag, der Abend, die Nacht, der Name, der Herr, die Frau, der Student / die Studentin, die Entschuldigung.

**Revision link to Lesson 0:** practise *heißen* (ei = "eye", ß = s), *wohnen* (silent h → long o), *Entschuldigung* (sch = sh, final -ung with [ŋ]), *Zürich* (z = ts, ü, ich-sound), *Studentin* (st- = sht).
