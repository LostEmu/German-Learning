# Reading Resources

Graded from beginner to academic. The level shown is the **earliest** point at which each becomes useful.

## Beginner → Elementary (A1–A2)

| Resource | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Graded readers** (*Lektüren*) from publishers such as Hueber, Klett and Cornelsen | Short stories written for a specific CEFR level, often with audio |
| **DW: Nicos Weg** texts and exercises | Accompanies the video course |

## Intermediate (B1)

| Resource | Notes |
|---|---|
| **nachrichtenleicht.de** (Deutschlandfunk) | Weekly news in simple German (*einfache Sprache*) |
| **DW: Top-Thema** | Short articles with vocabulary lists and audio |
| **LEIFIphysik** (leifiphysik.de) | German school-level physics explanations. Ideal first scientific reading; you already know the content |

## Upper intermediate (B2)

| Resource | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Welt der Physik** (weltderphysik.de) | Physics outreach articles |
| **German Wikipedia** (de.wikipedia.org) | Compare articles on topics you know (e.g. *Maxwell-Gleichungen*, *Fourier-Transformation*) with the English versions |
| **Spektrum der Wissenschaft** (spektrum.de) | Leading German popular-science magazine |
| **ETH News** (ethz.ch, German version) | Research news from ETH in Swiss Standard German |

## Advanced (C1)

| Resource | Notes |
|---|---|
| **NZZ** (Neue Zürcher Zeitung, nzz.ch) | Zurich's leading quality newspaper; Swiss Standard German, demanding register |
| **Tages-Anzeiger** (tagesanzeiger.ch) | Zurich daily newspaper |
| **German-language university textbooks** | e.g. introductory physics or numerical-methods textbooks used in German-language Bachelor programmes |

## How to read at each stage

1. **A1–A2:** read texts slightly *below* your level for fluency; look up very little.
2. **B1:** alternate *extensive* reading (for gist, no dictionary) and *intensive* reading (every word analysed).
3. **B2–C1:** read texts in your own field. Subject knowledge makes difficult German much easier to decode.
