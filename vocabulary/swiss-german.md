# Swiss German & Swiss Standard German

**Teaching plan:** Standard German (*Hochdeutsch*) is learned first. Systematic Swiss content starts around **B1/B2**. Until then this file only collects **recognition-only** items you are likely to notice.

---

## Key distinctions (to keep in mind from day one)

| Term | What it is | Where you meet it |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard German** (*Hochdeutsch*) | The standard written and formal spoken language of Germany, Austria and Switzerland | Everything we learn now |
| **Swiss Standard German** (*Schweizer Hochdeutsch*) | Standard German as written and spoken in Switzerland: same grammar, **no ß**, some different vocabulary (*Helvetismen*), Swiss pronunciation | Newspapers (e.g. NZZ), official letters, ETH administration, lectures in German, signs |
| **Swiss German** (*Schweizerdeutsch*) | A family of Alemannic **dialects** (e.g. *Züritüütsch*, Zurich German). Mainly spoken; no standard spelling | Everyday conversation in Zurich: shops, trams, colleagues, friends, local radio/TV |

**Diglossia:** German-speaking Swiss people generally *speak* dialect and *write* Standard German. Almost all of them can switch to Standard German when talking to someone who doesn't understand dialect, so Standard German is fully usable in Zurich from day one.

### Which skills matter where (overview, expanded at B1/B2)

| Context | Most useful |
|---|---|
| ETH academic environment | Master's teaching is mainly in **English**. German helps with administration, Bachelor-level lectures (largely in German), seminars, colleagues, and reading Swiss/German literature. Here **Standard German** is what you need |
| Official life (permits, contracts, letters) | **Swiss Standard German**, written |
| Everyday life (shops, neighbours, sport clubs, small talk) | Standard German works; **understanding** Zurich German makes daily life much easier and helps social integration |
| Long-term residence & integration | Standard German proficiency, ideally with comprehension of dialect. Swiss residence/naturalisation rules set formal language levels (*fide* framework). Check current requirements when relevant |

## Recognition-only items

### From Lesson 0: spelling
- **No ß in Switzerland:** *Strasse* (not *Straße*), *heissen*, *gross*. Same words, Swiss spelling.

### From Lesson 1: greetings (Zurich German, spelling varies)
| Swiss German | Standard German equivalent | Register |
|---|---|---|
| Grüezi | Guten Tag | formal, to one person |
| Grüezi mitenand | Guten Tag (zusammen) | formal, to a group |
| Hoi / Sali | Hallo | informal |
| Uf Wiederluege | Auf Wiedersehen | formal goodbye |
| Tschüss / Ciao | Tschüss | informal goodbye |
| Merci (vielmal) | Danke (vielmals) | very common Swiss "thanks" |

*Do not try to learn these actively yet. Mixing dialect into beginner Standard German causes confusion.*
