## 1. Infinitive, stem and ending *(Lesson 1)*

- The **infinitive** is the dictionary form. Almost all German infinitives end in **-en** (*kommen, wohnen, heißen*); a few end in **-n** (*sein*; later *tun, wandern*).
- The **stem** = infinitive minus *-en*: komm**en** → **komm-**.
- A **conjugated** verb = stem + an **ending** that agrees with the subject.

## 2. Present tense: *ich*, *du*, *Sie* *(Lesson 1)*

| Subject | Ending | kommen | wohnen | studieren | verstehen | heißen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ich | **-e** | komme | wohne | studiere | verstehe | heiße |
| du | **-st** | kommst | wohnst | studierst | verstehst | heiß**t** ⚠️ |
| Sie (formal) | **-en** | kommen | wohnen | studieren | verstehen | heißen |

**Rules and explanations**
- **Formal *Sie*** always uses the same form as the infinitive (for every verb except *sein*: *Sie sind*). Historically, *Sie* is the third-person plural *sie* ("they") used as a polite form, so it takes the plural verb form.
- **s-sound stems** (ending in -s, -ß, -z, -x): the *du* ending is only **-t**, because the *s* of *-st* merges into the stem: *du heiß**t***, not ~~*heißst*~~.

## 3. *sein* (to be) *(Lesson 1, partial)*

*sein* is irregular, so memorise it.

| Subject | Form |
|---|---|
| ich | **bin** |
| du | **bist** |
| Sie | **sind** |

*Remaining forms (er/sie/es ist, wir sind, ihr seid, sie sind) come in Lesson 2–3.*
