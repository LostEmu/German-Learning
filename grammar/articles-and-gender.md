## 0. Preview from Lesson 1

- Every German noun has one of three grammatical genders: **masculine (der)**, **feminine (die)**, **neuter (das)**.
- **Always learn a noun as: article + noun + plural.** *der Tag, die Tage*. The article is part of the word.
- All nouns are **capitalised**.

### Patterns already seen
| Pattern | Examples | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **-in** makes a female form of a person noun | der Student → die Student**in** (pl. -innen) | feminine; plural doubles the *n*: *-innen* |
| **-ung** | die Entschuldig**ung** | nouns ending in *-ung* are **always feminine** |

### No article with professions/roles after *sein*
*Ich bin Student.* / *Ich bin Studentin.*: no *ein/eine*. (English: "I am **a** student.")

### Countries
Most country names are neuter and used **without** an article (*Australien, Deutschland, Österreich*). A few have an obligatory article: **die Schweiz** → *aus der Schweiz, in der Schweiz* (explained with the dative, Lesson 24).
